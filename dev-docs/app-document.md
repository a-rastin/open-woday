Getting Started


A quick tour of Woday and how tasks, notes and your calendar fit together in one Mac app.

### One place for your day

Woday brings your to-dos, notes and calendar into a single Mac app. Instead of switching between three tools, you plan, write and schedule in one window.

Everything you create is stored locally on your Mac. There is no account to set up, so you can start working the moment the app opens.

### Your first five minutes

- Add a task and give it a due date
- Write a quick note and mention that task inside it
- Open the calendar to see your day at a glance
- Turn on iCloud sync in Settings if you use more than one Mac

That’s it. Explore the rest of these guides when you want to go deeper.

Getting Started


What you need to run Woday and how to install it on your Mac.

### Requirements

Woday requires macOS 26 (Tahoe) or later. It’s built natively for macOS with Apple’s latest frameworks, so it feels fast and at home on your Mac.

An iOS app is on the roadmap. For now, Woday runs on Mac only.

### Installing the app

Download Woday from the website, open the file and drag the app into your Applications folder. Launch it from there and you’re ready to go.

### Staying up to date

Woday checks for updates automatically and shows you a standard update window when a new version is ready. Nothing installs without your consent.

## Your first launch and the welcome tour

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/first-launch

All guides

Getting Started


What the four-page welcome tour asks you, which options it sets up and how to watch it again.

The first time you open Woday, a short welcome tour fills the window. It has four pages. Click **Continue** to move forward, or click the page dots to go back to a page you’ve already seen.

### The four pages

1. **Welcome to Woday**: a quick introduction. Click **Get Started**.
2. **Your license**: paste your license key and click **Activate**.
3. **Set things up**: choose a few options before you start. Everything here can be changed later in Settings.
4. **You’re all set**: a few tips and the **Start with sample content** switch. Click **Start Using Woday** to finish.

### Options on the setup page

- **Start at login**: Woday opens automatically when you log in to your Mac.
- **iCloud sync**: keeps your notes, tasks and events the same on every Mac signed in to your iCloud account. This takes effect the next time the app starts.
- **Apple Calendar**: shows your calendar’s events next to your own. Once macOS grants access, you can pick which calendars to include.
- **Apple Reminders**: your open reminders show up in Tasks, and checking one off completes it in Reminders too. You can pick which lists to include.
- **Apple Notes**: your Apple Notes appear as Woday notes, including lists, tables and images. You can pick which folders to sync.

macOS asks for permission the first time you turn on Calendar, Reminders or Notes.

### Watching the tour again

You can open the tour at any time from **Help ▸ Show Onboarding**.

## Sample content

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/sample-content

All guides

Getting Started


Load example notes, tasks and events to explore Woday, and remove them again without touching your own data.

Woday comes with a set of sample notes, tasks and events that show what the app can do. Each sample item carries a **Sample** badge, so you can always tell it apart from your own.

### On first launch

The last page of the welcome tour offers **Start with sample content**. It’s **off** by default, so a fresh install starts empty.

### Loading it later

1. Open **Settings ▸ Data**.
2. Click **Load** next to **Load demo data**.

This adds a fresh set dated around today. Any earlier sample copies are replaced, so you never end up with duplicates.

### Removing it

1. Open **Settings ▸ Data**.
2. Click **Delete…** next to **Delete demo data**.
3. Confirm with **Delete Demo Data**.

Only the sample items are removed. Your own notes, tasks and events stay, and your Apple Reminders are never touched. When Apple Notes sync is on and sample items are still around, **Settings ▸ Sync** also offers **Remove sample content**, which does the same thing.

Sample items are never copied to Apple Calendar, Apple Reminders or Google Calendar.

## The core ideas: tasks, events, notes, folders, tags and views

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/core-ideas

All guides

Getting Started


The handful of item types Woday is built from, and how they link to each other.

Woday is built from a few kinds of items, and they all work together.

- **Tasks**: things to do. They can have due dates and times, priorities, reminders, subtasks, durations and repeats (habits). A task can be waiting on someone, or saved for “someday”.
- **Events**: things that happen at a time, like meetings, appointments and all-day days. Events synced from Apple Calendar, Google Calendar or calendar feeds show up next to your own.
- **Notes**: rich documents built from blocks, including headings, lists, real to-do lines, tables, images, files, code and links. Every task and event also has its own note.

Tasks & Habits


Every way to add a task, from anywhere in the app or on the Tasks page, plus typing details into the title and opening and renaming tasks.

Tasks are the heart of Woday. A task can be as simple as a single line (“Buy milk”) or carry a due date and time, a reminder, a priority, a planned duration, subtasks, a location, attachments and a full note of its own. The Tasks & Habits articles explain everything you can do with a task and where to find it.

### Creating a task

There are several ways to add a task. Use whichever is closest to hand.

#### How to create a task from anywhere

1. Press ⌘N (or choose **File ▸ New Task**).
2. A new, untitled task is created for the selected day (today unless you picked another day) and opens in the right sidebar.
3. Type the title. The keyboard focus is already in the title field.

You can change the ⌘N shortcut in **Settings ▸ Shortcuts**.

#### How to add tasks on the Tasks page

1. Open **Tasks** in the sidebar (⌘5).
2. Click the **+** button in the page header to start a new line in the Inbox, or hover any group and click **+ Add task** at its end.
3. Type a title and press Return. The task is saved into that group: adding inside a day group gives it that day, adding inside a folder group files it there, and so on.
4. The field stays open, so you can type the next task right away. Press Esc when you are done.

While you type, a **⋯** button appears at the end of the line. It lets you set the **Due date**, **Priority**, **Reminder**, **Repeat as habit**, **Tags**, **Folder** and **Favorite** before the task is saved. If you have connected Apple Reminders, Todoist or a calendar, **Add to calendar** lets you send the new task straight to one of those lists or calendars.

#### Typing details right into the title

You can set most details without touching a menu, by typing them into the title: a date (“tomorrow 3pm”, “next week”, “someday”), `#tags`, a folder, a priority, a duration and a reminder. Woday recognises these as you type and shows them as chips under the title. See Quick-add syntax for the full list of words and symbols.

#### Other places that create tasks

- The **Inbox** in the right sidebar on the Today, Upcoming and Calendar pages has its own quick-add line.
- Writing a to-do line in any note turns it into a real task (it then appears on the Tasks page too). A to-do written in the Today page’s day note is due on that day.
- The command palette (⌘K) offers **New Task**.

### Opening a task

Click a task row to open it in the right sidebar (the **detail pane**). You can also hover a row and click the **›** button (**Open in sidebar**).

In the pane:

- Click the **↗** arrow (**Open in full view**) to open the task as a full page, with a larger title and more room for its note.
- Click the **×** to close the pane.

You can choose how tasks open by default in **Settings ▸ General ▸ Open tasks in**: **Right sidebar**, **Full screen** or **Overlay** (a centred card over a dimmed page).

#### Renaming a task

- In the detail pane, click the title and type.
- In a list, double-click the title to edit it in place. Single-clicking selects the row instead.

## Completing tasks and working with subtasks

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/completing-tasks-and-subtasks

All guides

Tasks & Habits


How to complete a task, the completion sound, and how to add, nest, view and complete subtasks.

### Completing a task

- Click the checkbox on any row, card or in the detail pane.
- Or choose **Mark as done** from the task’s **⋯** menu or right-click menu.

Completed tasks are crossed out and move to the **Done** group. To bring one back, uncheck it or choose **Mark as not done**.

Completing a task also clears its **In Progress** mark and ends any waiting (see Waiting).

The checkbox doubles as a progress indicator: when a task has subtasks, it fills as you complete them. Its colour reflects the task’s priority.

#### Completion sound

A short sound plays whenever you check off a task. To change it:

1. Open **Settings ▸ General**.
2. Turn **Completion sound** on or off.
3. Choose a **Sound**: **Pop**, **Tick**, **Chime**, **Bell** or **Melody**. Picking one plays a preview.

The sound only plays when *you* check something off, never when a task is completed by a sync or import.

### Subtasks

Any task can have subtasks, and subtasks can have their own subtasks.

#### How to add a subtask

- **From a list row:** hover the task and click the **+** button (**Add subtask**). A new subtask line opens under the task, ready for typing. Press Return to save it and start the next one; press Esc on an empty line to discard it.
- **From the task’s note:** in the detail pane, add a to-do line in the task’s note. Every to-do in a task’s note is a subtask of that task.
- **By dragging:** drag one task onto the middle of another task’s row. The row highlights, and on release the dragged task becomes its subtask.

#### Nesting and un-nesting

- To nest deeper, add a subtask to a subtask (hover it and click **+**), or indent a to-do line in the note with Tab.
- To pull a subtask out, drag it onto the **bottom edge** of another row. A short line shows where it will land, and the task becomes a sibling of that row instead of a child.

#### Seeing subtasks

- A task with subtasks shows a **done/total** badge (for example “2/5”) in its meta line. Click it to fold the subtasks open or closed right in the list (**Show subtasks** / **Hide subtasks**).
- In the detail pane, the subtasks appear as to-do lines in the task’s note, so you can check them off, reorder them and write around them.
- In the table layout, a chevron next to the title expands the subtasks as indented rows.

#### Completing subtasks

Check a subtask off like any task. The parent’s checkbox fills to show progress. A task with open subtasks counts as **In Progress** when you group by status.

When you delete a task, its subtasks go to the Trash with it.

### On iPhone

On the Woday iPhone app, tap a task to open it, swipe right on a row to **Complete** it (or **Reopen** it), and swipe left to **Delete** it. Long-press a row for quick actions such as **Due**, **Priority** and **Delete**.

## Due dates and scheduling

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/task-due-dates

All guides

Tasks & Habits


Schedule a task for a day, a time, a whole week, a whole month or Someday, and how overdue tasks and countdown badges work.

Every task can be scheduled at four levels of precision: a specific day (optionally with a time), a whole week, a whole month, or Someday.

### How to set a due date

1. Open the task’s **⋯** menu (hover a row) or right-click it, and choose **Due date**. In the detail pane, click the date badge, or **+ ▸ Due date**.
2. Pick one of:

- ****Today** / **Tomorrow****: Due on that day, no time
- ****This week** / **Next week****: Scheduled for the whole week
- ****Pick a week****: A later week from a list
- ****This month** / **Next month****: Scheduled for the whole month
- ****Pick a month****: A later month from a list
- ****Someday****: Parked with no date, deliberately
- ****Custom…****: Opens the date and time picker
- ****No date****: Removes the schedule (only shown when one is set)

### Custom dates and times

**Custom…** opens Woday’s date picker: a month calendar on the left and, when a time is set, a list of times on the right.

1. Click a day in the calendar.
2. Turn on **Time** to give the task a due time, then click a time in the list. You can also drag the highlighted time up or down to move it in 15-minute steps.
3. You can also click the date shown at the bottom and type something like “tomorrow 3pm”.
4. Click **Done**. **Clear** removes the due date.

A task with a due time appears on the Day, Days and Week calendar grids at that time.

### Week and month scheduling

Some tasks belong to “this week” or “in December” rather than a particular day. When you schedule a task for a week or month:

- It shows a period badge such as **This week**, **Next week**, **Week 47** or **December** instead of a date.
- On the Tasks page it is grouped under that period, after the individual days.
- In the Calendar’s Week view it appears in the **This week** strip above the grid; in the Month view it appears in the **This month** strip. You can drag tasks onto these strips to schedule them for the period.
- On the Today page, tasks for the current week appear in a collapsible **This week** card.
- It only becomes overdue when the week or month is over.

Weeks follow the **Week starts on** setting in **Settings ▸ General**.

### Someday

**Someday** is for tasks you have decided not to do now. A Someday task has no date, never becomes overdue, doesn’t show up in the Inbox, and is listed in its own **Someday** group at the end of the Tasks page. Its badge reads **Someday**; click it to reschedule. Setting a task to Someday removes its reminder.

### Overdue tasks

An open task whose due day (or due time) has passed is overdue. Overdue dates are shown in the accent colour, and overdue tasks gather in an **Overdue** group at the top of the Tasks page.

If you would rather not deal with yesterday’s leftovers by hand, turn on **Settings ▸ General ▸ Auto-move unfinished tasks**. Unfinished tasks then move to the next day by themselves at midnight (or the next time the app opens).

### Countdown badges

Tasks that are coming up show how much time is left, for example “45m left”, “3h left” or “2d left”.

- Set it from **⋯ ▸ Priority**, or from **+ ▸ Priority** in the detail pane.

### Waiting (Wait until…)

Sometimes a task is blocked on someone else: you’ve sent an email and are waiting for a reply. Instead of rescheduling it by hand, send the task to wait. It leaves your lists until the time you choose, then comes back as a follow-up.

#### How to send a task to wait

1. Open the task’s **⋯** menu and choose **Wait until**.
2. Pick a return time: **In 1 hour**, **In 3 hours**, **Tomorrow morning**, **In 2 days** or **In a week**. “Morning” means the start of your day, as set in **Settings ▸ General ▸ Day starts at**.
3. Or choose **Custom…** to open the **Wait until…** picker, where you can pick any future day and time.

- It wears an amber hourglass badge counting down to its return (for example “53m” or “3d”). Hover it to see the exact return time.
- It is listed in a **Waiting** group on the Tasks page and under **Waiting** in the right sidebar’s Inbox, instead of in its day.
- The **⋯** menu item reads **Waiting**. Use it to wait longer (the picker title becomes **Keep waiting until…**) or choose **Stop waiting** to bring the task back to today.

When the time is up, the task returns to your lists with a **Follow-up** badge (**Follow-up #2**, **#3**… if you have waited on it before), and you get a notification if **Settings ▸ Notifications ▸ Waiting tasks** is on.

Checking the task off ends the wait. Habits can’t be sent to wait, since they repeat on their own.

**Tip:** on the Tasks board, dragging a card into the **Waiting** column makes it wait until tomorrow morning. Dragging it into the **Inbox** or **In Progress** column stops the wait.

To see everything that is waiting, choose **Show ▸ Waiting** in the Tasks page’s filter menu, or pick **Waiting** from the right sidebar’s scope menu.

## Converting, duplicating and task history

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/convert-duplicate-history

All guides

Tasks & Habits


Turn a task into a note or an event and back, duplicate a task with its subtasks, and keep a dated history in the task’s note.

### Convert to another type

A task can become a note or an event, and back.

1. Open the task’s **⋯** menu and choose **Convert to**.
2. Pick **Note** or **Event** to convert it. The task’s content moves to the new item and the original task goes to the Trash.
3. Or, under **Keep original**, pick **Copy as note** or **Copy as event** to create the new item and leave the task untouched.

What carries over: the title, folder, tags, favorite star, cover and the note’s content. Converting a task to an event uses its due date and time (and duration, or one hour) as the event’s time; a task with a date but no time becomes an all-day event, and a task with no date starts at the next full hour. Location and reminder come along too, and open subtasks become to-do lines in the event’s note. Repeat rules are not carried over.

Events and notes have the same **Convert to** menu, so you can turn an event or a note into a task. The new item opens in the right sidebar of the page you’re on, when that page can show it.

### Duplicating

**Duplicate** in the **⋯** menu creates a copy of the task with the same title, details, due date and priority, including copies of its subtasks.

### Task history

Task history keeps a dated log of what happened to a task, right in its own note. It’s off by default.

1. Open **Settings ▸ General**.
2. Turn on **Task history**.

From then on, every change to a task (rescheduling, waiting and follow-ups, reminders, marking it done or reopening it, priority, folder and repeat changes) is added as a dated line under a **History** heading at the bottom of the task’s note. ## Writing basics in the block editor

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/writing-basics

All guides

Notes


How Return and blocks work, undo and redo, typing in any language, and notes on iPhone and iPad.

### Writing basics

- **Return** starts a new block. Pressing Return in the middle of a line splits it at the cursor, and the formatting moves with the text.
- In a bullet, numbered or to-do list, Return continues the list. Press Return on an **empty** list item to end the list.
- **Backspace** at the start of a line merges it into the line above. On a list item it first turns the item back into a paragraph. On an empty line it deletes the line.
- **↑** / **↓** at the first or last line of a block, and **←** / **→** at its very start or end, move the cursor to the neighbouring block.
- **Tab** indents a block one level and **⇧Tab** outdents it. A block can sit at most one level deeper than the block above it, up to 8 levels. Backspace on an empty indented line also steps it back one level.
- An empty note shows the hint *”Write, or type / for blocks…”*.

### Undo and redo

- **⌘Z** undoes and **⇧⌘Z** redoes your edits: typing, new, moved and deleted blocks, conversions, formatting and so on.
- Deleted to-dos and events go to the Trash, so you can restore them even after you’ve closed the note.

### Typing in any language

The editor fully supports input methods such as Chinese Pinyin, Japanese kana, Korean, and dead-key accents. While you’re composing a character, the line grows to fit and the placeholder hides, and the text is saved once you finish composing.

### On iPhone and iPad

The iOS app uses the same notes and blocks. Type `/` or use the block bar above the keyboard to insert Text, Heading, Bullet list, Numbered list, Task, Event, Note, Quote, Divider and Code blocks. The Markdown shortcuts (`# `, `- `, `[] `, `> `, `````, `---`, `1. `) work there too. Notes with other blocks, such as tables and images, still sync and display.

## Inserting blocks: the slash menu and Markdown shortcuts

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/inserting-blocks

All guides

Notes


Insert blocks with the slash menu, the + buttons, Markdown shortcuts, or by pasting text and links.

### The slash menu

Type `/` at the start of a line or after a space to open the **Blocks** menu. Keep typing to filter it (for example `/tab` for Table or `/todo` for Task), use ↑ / ↓ to move, and press Return to insert. Esc closes the menu.

The slash menu also works in the middle of a line. If you type `buy milk /todo`, that line becomes a to-do and keeps its text. Blocks that can’t hold text (divider, image, table and so on) are inserted **below** the line instead, so your words stay where they are. A `/` inside a word, such as a URL or a date like `11/10`, doesn’t open the menu.

### The + buttons

- Hover the gap **between two blocks** to reveal a small **+**. Click it to open the block menu and insert a block at that spot.
- Hover the empty area **below the last block** for a **+** that adds a block at the end. Clicking that empty area puts the cursor on a new line.

### Markdown shortcuts

Type one of these at the start of an empty paragraph and the line converts right away:

- **`# ` (or `## `, `### `)**: Heading
- **`- `, `* ` or `+ `**: Bullet list
- **`1. ` (any number followed by `. `)**: Numbered list
- **`[] ` or `[ ] `**: Task (to-do)
- **`> `**: Quote
- **`````**: Code block
- **`---`**: Divider

### Paste and links

- Pasting **multi-line text or Markdown** splits it into real blocks. Headings, bullets, numbered items, quotes, dividers, code fences and Markdown tables keep their types. `- [ ]` and `- [x]` lines become to-dos, and checked ones stay done. Indentation is kept, so nested to-dos become subtasks. This is handy for checklists copied from Notion and similar apps.
- Pasting a **single web link**, or pressing Return on a line that holds only a link, turns it into a **Link** bookmark card. YouTube and Vimeo links become a **Video** block instead.
- Single-line plain text pastes normally at the cursor.

## Task, event and note blocks

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/task-event-note-blocks

Notes


Write to-dos, events and notes as blocks right inside a note.

### Task blocks (to-dos)

A Task block is **a real task**, not just a checkbox. It shows up on the Tasks page, in Today and Upcoming when it has a date, in search, and everywhere else tasks appear.

- Click the checkbox to complete or uncomplete the task.
- Editing the line renames the task.
- **Indent** a to-do under another to-do (Tab) to make it a **subtask** of the to-do above. In a task’s own notes, every to-do you write is a subtask of that task. In an event’s notes, to-dos become tasks linked to the event.
- Type these symbols inside a to-do line to set task details, just like in quick add:
    - `@` sets the **due date**, for example `@tomorrow`
    - `!` sets the **priority**
    - `~` sets the **duration**
    - `$` files the task into a **folder**
    - `#` adds a **tag**
- Hover the row to show the **⋯** menu (**Open details** and the full task actions) and a chevron (**Open in sidebar**) that opens the task in the right rail.
- You can drag a task from the rail’s Inbox onto a to-do row to nest it there as a subtask.

If you turn a Task block into another kind, or delete it, its task moves to the Trash along with its subtasks. You can restore it from there.

### Event blocks

An Event block creates a real calendar event with the line’s text as its title. It starts at a default time: in a day’s notes it uses that day. The second line of the block shows the schedule and tags as chips you can click to reschedule or retag the event in place. Use the chevron (**Open in sidebar**) or the **⋯** menu (**Open details**) for the full event.

Events synced from other calendars (for example Apple Calendar) can appear as read-only lines in day notes. They show the time and calendar name, and a join button when the event has a meeting link.

## Magic Lines ✦: answers at the end of a line

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/magic-lines

All guides

Notes


Magic Lines puts a short answer at the end of a line as you write: the built-in calculator, AI answers, setup and what you’ll see.

Magic Lines puts a short, useful answer at the **end of a line** as you write: a calorie estimate, a price, a sum, a converted amount, a date, a correction, or a suggestion to turn the line into a task. The answer sits beside your text and isn’t part of it, so your writing stays clean. You can recompute or remove it at any time.

Magic Lines works in text, bullet, numbered, quote and to-do lines.

### The built-in calculator (always on)

These answers work in every editor with no setup and no AI. They appear only when the **whole line** is a calculation:

- **`25*18+120` or `250 + 15%`**: The result
- **`5 km in miles`**: `= 3.11 mi` (length, weight, volume and other units of the same kind)
- **`120 EUR in USD`**: The converted amount, using the day’s exchange rates (needs internet)
- **`today + 10 days`, `friday + 3 weeks`**: The resulting date

**Inline formulas:** end a calculation with `=` (for example `25*18+120 =`). The result appears in faint text right after the cursor. Press **Tab** to write it into the line. Keep typing, or press Space, and it goes away.

### Turning on AI answers

AI answers need an AI connection (see AI & MCP) or the **On-device** engine on Macs with Apple Intelligence. You can turn them on in two ways:

- **For one note:** choose **⋯ ▸ Magic Lines…**, or press ⌥⌘K. The first time, ⌥⌘K opens the setup. After that it turns Magic Lines on and off for the note.
- **Everywhere:** go to **Settings ▸ AI Connections** and turn on **Magic Lines everywhere**. The general **Helper** then runs in every note, task, event and day note. A note’s own Magic Lines setting always wins, so you can still switch it off for a single note.

### Setting it up

The **Magic Lines** sheet has an on/off switch and one question: **What should Wodie do with every line?** Describe it in your own words, or click a ready-made preset to fill in the text and then edit it:

- ****Helper****: Fixes spelling and factual mistakes, estimates amounts, adds one short useful fact or warning, and flags clear to-dos or meetings. Otherwise it stays silent (default)
- ****Calories****: Estimates calories for each food line, with a daily goal
- ****Budget****: Tracks expenses as minus and income as plus, in each line’s own currency, with a monthly budget
- ****Workout****: Estimates calories burned per exercise
- ****Protein****: Estimates grams of protein per food line
- ****Prices****: Estimates a typical price for each shopping item
- ****Time log****: How many minutes each activity takes
- ****Estimates****: Effort in hours for each planning line
- ****Language****: Translates words and phrases and corrects mistakes
- ****Travel****: Practical info: opening hours, tips, prices
- ****Meeting****: Flags action items and suggests next steps
- ****Facts****: One short, useful fact or correction per line
- ****Reading****: Author and a one-line summary for each book or article

If your description mentions a goal or unit (for example *”Calorie log, daily goal 2000 kcal”*), Woday picks it up and shows **Goal:** and **Unit:** below the text. Other options:

- **Activity**: **Quiet** (only values and local math), **Normal** (values, confident fixes, clear task/event flags), or **Active** (everything, plus short facts and tips).
- **AI engine**: **Auto** (*Picks for you*), **On-device** (*Private*), or a specific model.
- **Search the web**: lets Wodie look up live facts such as prices, opening hours or weather. This runs on Claude.

Click **Save** to apply your settings, **Cancel** to discard them, or **Remove** to delete the note’s Magic Lines setup.

### What you’ll see at the end of a line

- **A plain value, such as `~450 kcal`**: An answer that counts toward the totals. `~` marks an AI estimate. Calculator results have no `~`. What you can do: Click it to open the answer editor
- **A faint italic value with ⇥**: A suggestion. What you can do: **Tab** accepts it, **Esc** dismisses it
- **A struck-through value**: The line changed since the answer was calculated. What you can do: A new answer is on its way
- **A value with a red dot**: A value you typed yourself. What you can do: It’s never recalculated
- **`?`**: Wodie needs more detail. What you can do: Click it and give Wodie a hint, or type the value yourself
- **A shimmering pill or **Searching the web…****: Wodie is working. What you can do: Wait a moment

AI answers arrive as soon as you finish a line (press Return or move away), or after a short pause in typing. They don’t arrive on every keystroke.

## Magic Lines actions, sections, answers and totals

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/magic-lines-actions

All guides

Notes


Create tasks and events from a line, apply fixes and time logs, use sections and labels, edit answers and add up totals.

### Actions: tasks, events, fixes and time logs

Depending on your instructions and the **Activity** level, Wodie can also suggest an action:

- **Task:** a chip like **→ Task · tomorrow**. Click it to turn the line into a real Task block with the suggested date (and duration, when there is one).
- **Event:** a chip like **→ Event · Nov 20 14:00**. Click it to create a real event. An Event block is added below your line, and your line stays as it is.
- **Fix:** a short faint note at the end of the line when Wodie is confident the line has a mistake (a wrong date, name or number). Click it to see **Wodie’s correction**, then click **Apply fix** to replace the line or **Dismiss**.
- **Log time:** a chip like **+2 h → Logo design** when the line describes time spent on one of your open tasks. Click it to log that time in the task’s notes.
- **Which way should this go?** When a line is unclear, a quiet **N options** chip offers directions: Time log, Task, Estimate, Calories or Budget. Your pick applies to that line and to the rest of its section, down to the next blank line or label. Wodie remembers which directions you pick most and lists those first.

Esc dismisses a pending suggestion or chip for that line until you edit the line.

### Sections and labels

A heading, or a short label line ending with `:` such as `Expenses:`, `Time log:` or `Breakfast:`, tells Wodie what the lines below it are about. Each section is handled in its own way, so one note can mix a time log, expenses and income. Label lines never get an answer themselves. Headings show a **subtotal** of the confirmed values in their section.

### The answer editor

Click any answer to open the answer editor. You can:

- Type your own **Value**. Woday keeps it and won’t recalculate it.
- Read the breakdown, when there is one.
- Use **Tell Wodie what you meant…** to add a hint and get a new answer.
- Click **Remember: …** to teach Wodie a value, such as your usual breakfast. Remembered values are listed in **Settings ▸ AI Connections**, where you can remove them.
- Choose **Recompute**, **Insert as text** (adds the value to the line’s text), or the trash button (**Delete answer**).
- Click **Back** to choose a different direction, if the answer came from the options chooser.

### Totals

Only confirmed values are added up, and Woday does the adding itself, not the AI. Each unit gets its own badge in the note’s badge row, for example **7 h** and **1 025 kcal**. Budget badges show the net amount. Click a badge for the total, today’s amount, your goal and how much is left (or how far over you are), plus a breakdown by heading. The **✦ Magic Lines** badge shows which preset is running. Click it to open the settings. Tasks and events show the same badges in their panes.

When you export a note to Markdown, confirmed answers are included as `line — value`, and the total is added at the end.

### Setting the date and time

All of an event’s timing lives in **one schedule badge**, such as “Wed 19 Nov · 9:00 – 10:00” or “Wed 19 Nov · All-day”. Click it to open the start/end picker:

1. The two dates at the bottom are the **start** and the **end**. Click one to choose which end you’re editing (you can also type into it, for example “tomorrow 3pm”).

## Quick Add: where you can create items by typing

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/quick-add-overview

All guides

Quick Add


Every place that understands plain sentences, and the difference between natural language and symbol menus.

Woday understands plain sentences. Type something like `Dentist friday 14:00` or `T: Pay rent october 1 !!! #home` and Woday works out what to create, when it happens, and which tags, folder and priority it gets. Recognized words are highlighted as you type, and a preview shows the result before you press Return.

### Where you can quick-add

- **Top-bar search field** (⌘K): anything, a task, event, note or habit.
- **Quick Search launcher** (⌥Space): anything on the Search tab, or that tab’s kind of item on Today, Tasks, Events, Calendar and Notes.
- **Tasks page**: hover a group and click **+ Add task** to create tasks and habits.
- **Events page**: hover a group and click **+ Add event**.
- **Right rail**: the capture line at the top of the Inbox, Calendar or day view. Click the marker at the start of the line to choose Task, Event or Notes.
- **iPhone and iPad**: the Search, or create anything… field and the New task… / New event… lines.

Change the ⌥Space and ⌘K shortcuts in **Settings ▸ Shortcuts** (Quick Search and Focus Search Field).

### Two styles of input

- **Natural language**: plain words and simple symbols like `tomorrow`, `14:00`, `#work`, `!!` and `every monday`. They’re read from the whole sentence when you create the item. The full set works in the top bar, the launcher and on iOS; the Tasks and Events pages read them when you press Return.
- **Symbol menus**: in the Tasks, Events and rail lines and the launcher’s capture tabs, typing a symbol like `#` or `@` opens a small menu to pick from.

### Quick reference

- `T:` `task:` `todo:` create a task, `E:` `event:` an event, `N:` `note:` a note, `H:` `habit:` a habit.
- `#name` adds a tag (created if new). `tag name` does the same as a word.
- `$name` files it in an existing folder.
- `!` `!!` `!!!` set Low, Medium or High priority.
- `today`, `tomorrow`, `tonight`, weekday names and dates like `october 23` set a day.
- `14:00`, `9:30`, `2pm` set a time.
- `this week`, `next month`, `week 45`, `october` plan for a period; `someday` parks it.
- `every …`, `daily`, `weekly`, `on mondays` set a repeat and make a habit.
- `@` before a date word is optional: `@tomorrow` equals `tomorrow`.

## Choosing whether you create a task, event, note or habit

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/quick-add-choosing-the-kind

All guides

Quick Add


Kind prefixes, kind words, command sentences, automatic guessing and the kind badge.

### Kind prefixes

Start your text with a prefix to say exactly what you want:

- `T:`, `task:`, `todo:` → Task
- `E:`, `event:` → Event
- `H:`, `habit:` → Habit
- `N:`, `note:` → Note

The prefix only counts as the **first word**, so a `Re:` later in the sentence is left alone. Capitals and the space after the colon don’t matter: `T:Call garage` and `t: Call garage` both work. With a prefix, all your other words stay in the title.

### Kind words

These words anywhere in the sentence choose the kind and are removed from the title: `task`, `todo`, `to-do`, `reminder` (task), `event` (event), `note`, `notes` (note). For example, `note grocery list` creates a note titled “grocery list”.

Watch out: without a prefix, the first kind word is treated as the kind. `Write release note` becomes a note called “Write release”. Write `T: Write release note` to keep the word.

### Command sentences

If your sentence has a kind word **and** a verb like `create`, `new`, `add` or `make`, Woday treats it as a command and also drops filler words such as a, an, the, with, for, use, title, titled, called and named.

- `create new event tomorrow 14:00 called Budget review` → event “Budget review”, tomorrow 14:00–15:00.
- `add a task for the dentist friday` → task “dentist”, due Friday.

### When you don’t say the kind

1. A repeat rhythm (`every monday`, `daily`) → Habit.
2. An event word: `meeting`, `call`, `lunch` or `appointment` → Event. The word stays in the title.
3. A time (`14:00`, `3pm`, `tonight`) → Event.
4. Anything else, with or without a date → Task.

A task word with a rhythm is also a habit: `task water plants every 3 days`.

### The kind badge

In the top bar and on the launcher’s Search tab, a colored badge at the end of the field shows what Return will create: **Task** (blue), **Event** (red), **Note** (yellow) or **Habit** (orange). Click it to choose another kind; your choice applies until you clear the field. With AI set up, the menu also has **Ask Wodie**. Text that looks like a question goes to Wodie automatically, so pick a kind from the badge if you meant to create an item.

## Typing dates and times

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/quick-add-dates-and-times

All guides

Quick Add


Day words, specific dates, times and what a date does to each kind of item.

### Days

- `today`, `tomorrow`: that day.
- `tonight`: today at 20:00.
- `monday` … `sunday`: the **next** occurrence, starting from tomorrow. On a Monday, `monday` means next week’s Monday. `next monday` is the same.
- `october 23`, `oct 23`, `oct 23rd`: the next October 23, this year or next year if it has passed.
- `23 october`, `1st march`: the same with the day first.
- `oct 23 2027`, `23 october 2027`: that exact date (years 2000–2099).

Month names can be full or short (jan, feb, mar, apr, jun, jul, aug, sep/sept, oct, nov, dec), and days can have an ending like 1st or 23rd. Impossible dates such as `february 31` stay in the title. Write weekdays in full as plain words (`friday`, not `fri`). Numeric dates like `10/23` or `2026-10-23` aren’t recognized.

### Times

- `14:00`, `9:30`, `0:15`: 24-hour time.
- `2pm`, `11am`, `12pm`, `12am`: 12-hour time on the hour. `12pm` is noon, `12am` is midnight.

Write am/pm right after the number (`5pm`, not `5 pm`). For minutes, use the 24-hour form: `14:30` instead of `2:30pm`. A time without a day means today. The words `at` and `on` right before a date or time are dropped, so `Call Anna on friday at 10:00` gives the title “Call Anna”.

### What the date does

- **Task / habit**: due at that day and time, or that day. With nothing, no due date (habits start today).
- **Event**: with a time it starts then and lasts 1 hour. With a date only it’s all-day that day. With nothing, all-day today.
- **Note**: the date isn’t used.

To give an event a different length, use the date badge in the Events page or rail quick-add line, which opens a start–end picker, or edit the event afterwards.

Schedule a task for a whole period instead of a single day.

## Typing repeating items (habits)

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/quick-add-repeating-items

All guides

Quick Add


Rhythm phrases that turn a task into a habit, and how the start date is chosen.

A repeat rhythm turns a task into a **habit**, a task that comes back on a schedule. Without an explicit kind, Woday makes a habit automatically when it sees a rhythm.

### Rhythm phrases

- `daily`, `every day`: every day.
- `weekly`, `every week`: every week.
- `monthly`, `every month`: every month.
- `yearly`, `annually`, `every year`: every year.
- `biweekly`, `fortnightly`, `every other week`, `every 2 weeks`, `every second week`: every 2 weeks.
- `every 3 days`, `every two months`, `every 10 weeks`: any number from 1 to 99, as digits or words (one … ten, twelve).
- `every monday`: weekly on Monday.
- `every mon, wed and fri`, `every tue & thu`: weekly on those days. `and`, `&`, `+` and commas all work.
- `every other friday`: every 2 weeks on Friday.
- `every weekday`, `every workday`: Monday to Friday. `every weekend`: Saturday and Sunday.
- `on mondays`, `on mondays and thursdays`, `on weekdays`, `on weekends`: weekly on those days.

After `every`, weekdays can be full, short (mon, tue/tues, wed, thu/thur/thurs, fri, sat, sun) or plural. `each` works like `every`. A plural weekday is a rhythm only after `every` or `on`, so `sundays brunch ideas` keeps “sundays” in the title. The word “habit” inside the text doesn’t create a habit; use `H:`.

Leading rhythm words stay in the title: `Weekly review` sets the rhythm and keeps the word. In `Review finances monthly` the word is removed.

### When the habit starts

- With a date, such as `Swim weekly oct 1`, it starts on that date. Otherwise today.
- With weekdays, the start moves forward to the first matching day. `H: Gym every mon and thu` typed on a Tuesday starts on Thursday.
- `H:` with no rhythm repeats daily.

### Events and notes don’t repeat

If you explicitly ask for an event or note, rhythm words stay in the title. `E: Every Monday matters` gives an event titled “Every Monday matters”.

## Symbol menus: #, @, !, ~, *, $ and /

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/quick-add-symbol-menus

Quick Add


The pickers that open when you type a symbol in the Tasks, Events and rail lines and the launcher tabs.

In the right rail, use the menus; plain words like `tomorrow` stay in the title there, and the **⋯** button offers repeat and favorite.

## Other languages and Quick Add examples

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/quick-add-languages-and-examples

All guides

Quick Add


Date and repeat words in Spanish, German, French and Hungarian, plus a set of worked examples.

Date and repeat words are also understood in **Spanish, German, French and Hungarian**, whatever language the app uses. Accents are optional (`manana` = mañana, `hetfo` = hétfő).

- **today**: hoy, heute, aujourd’hui, ma
- **tomorrow**: mañana, morgen, demain, holnap
- **this / next week**: esta / próxima semana, diese / nächste Woche, cette / prochaine semaine, ezen / jövő héten
- **this / next month**: este / próximo mes, diesen / nächsten Monat, ce / prochain mois, ebben / jövő hónapban
- **every**: cada, jeden/jede/jedes, chaque, minden
- **daily, weekly, monthly, yearly**: diariamente…, täglich, wöchentlich, monatlich, jährlich, quotidiennement…, naponta, hetente, havonta, évente
- **someday**: valamikor (Hungarian)

Weekday and month names are recognized in all five languages, for example `23 octubre`, `5 mai`, `14 juillet`, `március 15`. 今天 and 明天 work too. Kind prefixes, kind words and `tonight` are English only.

### Examples

- `Buy milk tomorrow` → task due tomorrow.
- `T: Call mom tomorrow 5pm !! #family` → task at 17:00, Medium priority, tag family.
- `Call mom tomorrow 5pm #family` → event, because “call” suggests one.
- `Dentist friday 14:00` → event 14:00–15:00. `Dentist friday` → task due Friday.
- `E: Mia’s birthday march 14` → all-day event.
- `N: Packing list #travel $Trips` → note with a tag, in the Trips folder.
- `Pay rent october 1 !!! #home` → High priority task.
- `Read that book someday` → task on the Someday list.
- `Tax return week 15` → task for calendar week 15.
- `Pick up dry cleaning tonight` → event today at 20:00. With `T:` it’s a task due at 20:00.
- `Gym every mon, wed and fri 7:00` → habit on those days at 7:00.
- `Weekly review friday` → habit “Weekly review”, starting Friday.
- `E: Standup every day 9:30` → event “Standup every day”, because events don’t repeat.
- `Zahnarzt morgen 10:00` → event tomorrow 10:00.

### Tips

- Check the highlights and the Create row before pressing Return. Anything not highlighted is the title.
- A word taken as a date or kind that belongs in the title? Start with a prefix like `T:`.
- Use `14:30`, not `2:30pm`.
- The date fields in Woday’s pickers accept the same phrases, like `next friday` or `oct 23 9:00`.

A folder can hold notes, tasks, events and files. Each item is in at most one folder.

### How to create a folder

1. In the sidebar, hover the **Folders** section header and click **+** (New folder).

3. Type a name and press Return.

### How to put items into a folder

You have several options:

- **Drag** a task, event or note card or row onto a folder in the sidebar. The folder row gets a ring while you hover. Release to file the item.
- **Right-click** an item:
    - Notes: **Move to** ▸ choose a folder, or **No folder**.
    - Tasks and events: **Folder** ▸ choose a folder, or **No folder**.
- In a task’s or event’s detail pane, click **+** ▸ **Folder** and pick one. Once set, the folder shows as a badge you can click to change it.

- **Create the item inside the folder.** On a folder’s page, click **+** and choose **New note**, **New task**, **New event** or **Add file…**. The new item is filed in that folder.

### The folder page

Click a folder’s name in the sidebar to open its page. It shows everything in the folder (notes, tasks, events and files) in one list.

- **Name and icon:** click the title to rename the folder in place. Click the icon next to it to pick a different one.
- **+ menu:** **New note**, **New task**, **New event**, **Add file…**. New items are filed here.
- **⋯ menu:** **Rename…**, **Add cover** / **Change cover** / **Remove cover**, **Add to Favorites** / **Remove from Favorites**, **Delete**.
- **Settings button** (the slider icon): layout, grouping, sort, filters and visible fields. See Filters, grouping and sorting.
- **View tabs:** pill tabs under the title let you save several setups for this folder. See Views.

### Folders in the sidebar

- Click the **name** to open the folder’s page.
- Click the **chevron** to show the folder’s notes right in the sidebar. You see up to five, then **View all (N)**, which opens the folder.
- The sidebar lists five folders. If you have more, **View all (N)** opens the Folders page, which shows all of them as tiles or a list.
- Right-click a folder for **Open**, **Rename…**, cover options, **Add to Favorites**, and **Delete**.

### How to delete a folder

1. Right-click the folder (or open its ⋯ menu) and choose **Delete**.
2. Confirm. Woday tells you: “Its notes and tasks are kept and become unfiled.”

Nothing inside the folder is deleted. Its items simply have no folder afterwards. The folder’s own saved views are deleted along with it.

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/the-inbox

Your “not dealt with yet” pile in the right rail, and how items leave it.

The Inbox is your “not dealt with yet” pile. It lives in the right rail. Choose **Inbox** from the rail’s title menu (see The right rail).

- ****Filter****: **Types**. Choices: **Notes**, **Tasks**, **Events**, **Files** (any combination)
- **Status**. Choices: **All**, **Open**, **Waiting**, **Done**
- **Due**. Choices: **All**, **Overdue**, **Today**, **Tomorrow**, **This week**, **Next 7 days**, **Next 30 days**, **This month**, **Has a date**, **No date**

- **Folders**. Choices: Pick any number of folders. Not shown on a folder page, where the folder already sets the scope.

- ****Display****: **Fields**. Choices: **Date**, **Priority**, **Location**, **Folder**, **Tags**: which details appear on cards, rows and table columns. The name always shows.

- **Status, Priority and Source apply to tasks only.** If you set one, notes and events drop out of the results, because they don’t have a status, priority or source.
- **Due** uses each item’s date: a task’s due date, an event’s start, a note’s date.
- **Sort by Default** keeps the natural order. Sorting by due date mixes all item types into one timeline. Items without a date go last.
- **Group by + Cards** gives side-by-side columns, like a board. **Group by + List** gives collapsible sections. The **Table** layout is always one flat, sortable table.
- With **Table**, click a column header (title, date, priority) to sort by it. Click the same header again to reverse the order.

Next to the settings button is a small chevron (**Show active filters**). Click it to open a row of pills under the title, one for every filter option. Options you’ve set show their value. Unset ones show as dashed placeholders. Click any pill to change it, or choose **Remove** to clear it. Click the chevron again to hide the row.

### Tasks, Events, Notes, Upcoming and Calendar pages

These pages use a simpler version of the same button. Depending on the page, you’ll find:

- **Layout**, for example **List**, **Board** and **Table** on the Tasks page.
- **Arrange**: **Group by** and **Sort by**, with options that fit the page.
- **Filter**: **Show** (such as All / Upcoming / Overdue / Waiting / Done on the Tasks page), **Tags**, and **Folders** (each with an **All** option at the top).
- **Sources**: **Show completed tasks**, **Show habits**, **Show synced events** (with per-calendar choices), **Show synced reminders** (with per-list choices), and **Show Todoist tasks** / **Show Linear tasks** when those are connected.
- **Reset Filters**, which appears once anything is changed.

The **Show completed tasks** and **Show habits** switches are shared across pages. If you change them on one page, every page follows.

## Drag and drop

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/drag-and-drop

All guides

Organizing & Views


Drag tasks, events and notes between lists, boards, calendars and the sidebar, what each drop does, and reordering the sidebar.

Almost everything in Woday can be dragged. Drag a task, event or note card or row from any list, board, calendar, table or the rail, and drop it where you want it. A ring or tint shows what will receive the drop.

- You can drag task, event and note rows and cards from any page and from the rail. In tables, use the grip that appears at the left edge of a row on hover, or drag the row itself.
- Events synced from Apple Calendar or Google Calendar can’t be dragged.

### What happens when you drop

- **A **folder** in the sidebar, on the Folders page, or a starred folder card on the Favorites page**: A task: Filed in that folder. An event: Filed in that folder. A note: Filed in that folder
- **A **tag** in the sidebar**: A task: Gets the tag (never removed). An event: Gets the tag. A note: Gets the tag
- **A **saved view** in the sidebar**: A task: Gets the view’s folder, tags or star, so it appears in the view (see Views). An event: Same. A note: Same
- **The rail in a **Folder** scope**: A task: Filed in that folder. An event: Filed in that folder. A note: Filed in that folder
- **The rail in a **Tag** scope**: A task: Gets the tag. An event: Gets the tag. A note: Gets the tag
- **The rail in **Inbox** (or another list scope)**: A task: Due date cleared, so it’s back in the planning pile. Its folder is kept. An event: Nothing changes (events always have a date). A note: Date cleared
- **A day in the rail’s **Calendar** month grid, or the day list under it**: A task: Moved to that day, keeping its time. An event: Moved to that day, keeping its time. A note: Placed on that day
- **The rail’s **Today** hour grid**: A task: Scheduled at that time (snaps to 15 minutes). An event: Moved to that time, keeping its length. A note: Placed on that day
- **A day on the **Today** page, in **Upcoming**, in the **Calendar**, or a day section on the **Events** page**: A task: Moved to that day. An event: Moved to that day. A note: Placed on that day
- **A column on the **Tasks** board**: A task: Gets that column’s value: due day, Inbox (date cleared), Someday, Done, priority, note or status
- **Another **task** card or row**: A task: Becomes its subtask. An event: Linked to the task. A note: Linked to the task
- **An **event** card**: A task: Linked to the event. A note: Becomes the event’s note
- **A **note** card**: A task: Added to the end of the note as a line, and linked. An event: Added to the end of the note as a line. A note: Added as a link line

A few deliberate limits:

- Dropping onto a saved view never changes status, due date or priority. A drop won’t complete a task or change how important it is.
- The Inbox drop leaves the folder alone on purpose, so you can send a filed task back for re-planning without losing its folder.
- The **Favorites** sidebar section only reorders. Dropping items onto it doesn’t star them. Use **Add to Favorites** instead.

### Reordering the sidebar

You can arrange **folders** and **tags** by hand:

1. Drag a folder (or tag) row up or down in its sidebar section.
2. An accent line shows where it will go. Release to drop it there.

The order is used in the sidebar, on the Folders and Tags pages, and in the rail’s Folder and Tag menus. It syncs to your other devices.

**Pinning:** the sidebar shows the first five folders and tags. To keep one always visible, drag it into the top five. New folders and tags appear at the top.

The Favorites section reorders the same way (that order stays on this Mac). Saved views in the sidebar are not reordered by dragging.

You can also choose which sidebar sections show, and in what order, in **Settings ▸ Appearance**. Use ↑ and ↓ to move a section and the switch to hide it. The same settings page controls which pages appear in the sidebar menu. The Folders, Tags and Views pages are hidden from the menu by default, but the sections’ **View all** links and search still reach them.

## Dropping files from Finder

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/dropping-files-from-finder

All guides

Organizing & Views


Drag files from Finder onto a folder, tag or other place to import them right there.

Drag files from Finder onto a folder or tag, and Woday imports them right there:

- **Markdown (.md) and text (.txt) files** become notes, with headings, lists and checkboxes turned into real blocks and tasks.
- **Images, PDFs and any other file** are added as file items you can preview and open with Quick Look.
- **CSV and Excel files** are skipped, because tables need a column mapping. Use **Settings ▸ Import** for those.

### Where you can drop files

- **A folder in the sidebar**: In that folder
- **A tag in the sidebar**: Tagged with that tag
- **A folder tile on the Folders page, or a starred folder card on the Favorites page**: In that folder
- **A tag tile on the Tags page, or a starred tag card on the Favorites page**: Tagged with that tag
- **Anywhere on an open folder or tag page**: In that folder, or with that tag
- **The **Files** row in the sidebar, or the Files page**: Unfiled (in the Inbox)
- **The right rail**: In the rail’s folder or tag scope. Otherwise unfiled.

## What views are and what they remember

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/views-overview

All guides

Organizing & Views


The settings a saved view remembers, and how where you create a view decides what it looks at.

A **view** is a saved way of looking at your items: a filter, a grouping, a sort order and a layout, kept under a name. Make a “This week” view, a “Waiting on others” view, or a table of every open high-priority task, and get back to it in one click. Views update live as your tasks, events and notes change. You can open them from the sidebar, keep several as tabs on a folder or page, and even embed one inside a note.

### What a view remembers

- ****Layout****: **Cards**, **List**, **Table**
- ****Group by****: **None**, **Type**, **Status**, **Date**, **Priority**, **Tag**
- ****Sort by****: **Due date**, **Created**, **Modified**, **Priority**, **Title**, **Default**, plus **Descending**
- ****Types****: **Notes**, **Tasks**, **Events**, **Files**
- ****Status****: **All**, **Open**, **Waiting**, **Done**
- ****Due****: **All**, **Overdue**, **Today**, **Tomorrow**, **This week**, **Next 7 days**, **Next 30 days**, **This month**, **Has a date**, **No date**
- ****Priority****: **All**, **High**, **Medium**, **Low**, **None**
- ****Source****: **All**, **Reminders** (synced Apple Reminders), **Recurring** (repeating tasks and habits)
- ****Tags****: Any number of tags. Items with at least one of them match.
- ****Folders****: Any number of folders (on views that aren’t tied to a single folder)
- ****Hide completed****: On or off
- ****Fields****: Which details show on cards, rows and table columns: **Date**, **Priority**, **Location**, **Folder**, **Tags**

A view also has a **name**, an **icon** and, optionally, a **cover image**.

How the filters combine:

- An item must match **every** filter you set. For example, Tasks + Open + Overdue means “open tasks that are overdue”.
- **Status**, **Priority** and **Source** apply to tasks only. Setting any of them leaves notes and events out.
- Date filters are relative. A “Today” view always means *today*, not the day you saved it. **This week** covers today through the end of the current calendar week.
- **Due** uses a task’s due date, an event’s start, and a note’s date.

### Kinds of views

Where you create a view decides what it looks at:

- ****Global view****: Created from: The **+** on the sidebar’s **Views** section, or on the Views page. Looks at: All your items. Shows up: Sidebar **Views** list, Views page, search, note view blocks
- ****Folder view****: Created from: The **+** next to the tabs on a folder’s page. Looks at: That folder’s items. Shows up: As a tab on the folder page, in the sidebar **Views** list, Views page, note view blocks
- ****Tag view****: Created from: The **+** next to the tabs on a tag’s page. Looks at: Items with that tag. Shows up: As a tab on the tag page, in the sidebar **Views** list, Views page, note view blocks
- ****Favorites view****: Created from: The **+** next to the tabs on the Favorites page. Looks at: Your starred items. Shows up: Only as a tab on the Favorites page
- ****Page view****: Created from: The **+** next to the tabs on the Tasks, Events or Notes page. Looks at: That page. Shows up: As a tab on that page, in the sidebar **Views** list, Views page

Every folder, tag, Favorites, Tasks, Events and Notes page always has at least one tab, its **default view**. It’s the first tab, and it can’t be deleted.

### Views as tabs on folder, tag and Favorites pages

Folder, tag and Favorites pages have a row of **pill tabs** under the title. Each tab is a view of that page’s items.

1. Open the folder, tag or Favorites page.

#### Working with tabs

- **Switch:** click a tab. The page instantly shows that view’s layout, grouping, sort and filters.
- **Right-click a tab** for:
    - **Rename**: type a new name and press Return. Leave it empty to go back to the layout name.

- ****Tasks****: **Show** filter (All, Upcoming, Overdue, Waiting, Done), **Group by**, **Sort by**, **Descending**, and layout (**List**, **Board**, **Table**)
- ****Events****: **Show** filter (Upcoming, Past, All), **Group by**, **Sort by**, **Descending**, and layout (**List**, **Board**, **Table**)
- ****Notes****: Sort order (**Edited**, **Created**, **A–Z**) and layout (**Cards**, **List**, **Table**)

A new page tab starts from the page’s “everything, newest first” state. Tag and folder filters on these pages aren’t part of the tab.

## Embedding a view in a note

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/embedding-views

All guides

Organizing & Views


Place a live view inside a note, use its block controls, and what happens when the view is renamed or deleted.

You can place a live view inside any note. It’s handy for a project note that always shows the project’s open tasks, or a weekly review page.

### How to insert a view block

1. In a note, type `/` on an empty line and choose **View** (Embed a saved view). You can also type `/view`.
2. Click **Choose a view** and pick one from the list. Folder views are shown as *Folder › View name*.

Global, folder and tag views can be embedded. Page views and Favorites views aren’t in the list, because they only make sense on their own pages. If you haven’t saved any views yet, the menu says “No saved views yet”.

### What you see

The block shows the view’s name and icon, then its matching items, laid out like the view itself (**Cards**, **List** or **Table**, with groups if the view is grouped):

- **It’s live.** Items appear, change and disappear as you work. Checking off a task in the block really completes it.
- **Click an item** to open it.
- **Drag items** out of the block like from any other list.
- The block starts with **5 items**. **View more (N)** loads 15 more each time, and **Show less** goes back to 5. Very long results scroll inside the block instead of stretching the note.
- If nothing matches, it says “Nothing matches this view yet.”

### Block controls

All of these are in the block’s header:

- ****Chevron** (left)**: Collapses the block to just its title, or expands it
- ****↗ arrow** (appears on hover)**: **Open as full page**: jumps to the view’s page
- ****Settings button** (slider icon)**: Changes the layout, **Group by**, **Sort by** (with **Descending**), and the **Status**, **Due**, **Priority**, **Source** and **Hide completed** filters, right inside the note
- ****Chevron** (right, when filters are set)**: Shows or hides pills naming the active filters
- ****⋯****: Switch the block to another view, or **Remove** the block

Changes made in the block are temporary at first:

- Click **Save** to write them back into the view. This updates the view everywhere, including its page and other blocks.
- Click **✕** (Discard these changes) or **Reset** to go back to the view as saved.

The block keeps the view’s type, tag and folder filters as they are. Change those on the view’s own page.

### When a view is renamed or deleted

- **Renamed:** the block picks up the new name automatically.
- **Deleted:** the block shows “This view was deleted”, with the old name and “pick another”. Click it to choose a different view, or **Remove** the block.

### How to search and open something

1. Press ⌘K.
2. Start typing.
3. Use ↑ and ↓ to move through the results. Hovering with the mouse selects a row too.
4. Press Return to open the selected result, or click it.
5. Press Esc to close the dropdown.

### Item actions (Tab)

- **Task**: Open, Open in Full View, Mark as Done / Mark as Not Done, Reschedule to Today, Reschedule to Tomorrow, Move to Trash.
- **Event**: Open, Open in Full View, Move to Trash.
- **Note**: Open, Move to Trash.

Type to filter the actions, press Return to run one, and press Esc to go back to the results.

### Command mode

Start the query with `>` to list only commands. For example, `>theme` shows every theme command and `>sync` shows every sync command. You don’t need the prefix to find commands: matching commands also appear in normal results under **Commands**.

- **Navigation**: Go to Today, Upcoming, Calendar, Notes, Tasks, Events, Habits, Folders, Tags, Views, Analytics or Trash, Open AI Chat, Open Settings.
- **Create**: New Task, New Note, New Event.
- **View**: Toggle Light / Dark, the Theme commands, Text Size: Small / Default / Large, Time Format: System / 12-hour / 24-hour.
- **Sync**: Sync Now, Refresh Apple Calendar, Refresh Calendar Feeds, plus Sync Google Calendar, Todoist, Linear, Notion and Obsidian once connected.
- **Data**: Export All Data (CSV), Import Data…, Connect Google Calendar.
- **Settings**: one Settings command for every section, such as Settings: Appearance.

### Asking Wodie from the search field

Once you’ve added an AI key, text that looks like a question (it contains a `?` or starts with a question word) switches the field to **Ask Wodie about your tasks, notes and calendar**. Press Return to get an answer in the dropdown, then choose **Open in Chat** to continue or **Copy** to copy it. The badge at the right end of the field shows what Return will do; click it to switch between creating a task, event or note, or asking Wodie.

### Supported providers

- **Claude (Anthropic)**: Where to get a key: console.anthropic.com ▸ API keys. Key looks like: `sk-ant-…`. Models in the picker: Claude Opus, Claude Sonnet, Claude Haiku
- **ChatGPT (OpenAI)**: Where to get a key: platform.openai.com ▸ API keys. Key looks like: `sk-…`. Models in the picker: GPT-6.1 Terra, GPT-6.1, GPT-5.1, GPT-5 mini
- **Gemini (Google)**: Where to get a key: aistudio.google.com ▸ API keys. Key looks like: `AIza…`. Models in the picker: Gemini 3.1 Pro, Gemini 3 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash Lite
- **Grok (xAI)**: Where to get a key: console.x.ai ▸ API keys. Key looks like: `xai-…`. Models in the picker: Grok 4, Grok 3 mini

### How to add an API key

1. Open **Settings ▸ AI Connections** and click the **AI Assistant** card.
2. Turn on the switch next to the provider you want (for example **Claude**).
3. Paste your key into the field that appears.
4. Click **Save**. Once the key is stored, the button reads **Saved**.

To stop using a provider for a while, turn its switch off. Woday keeps the key, so you can turn it back on later without pasting it again. To remove a key completely, click the trash button (**Delete key**) next to it.

### Where your key is stored

Your API keys are stored in the **macOS Keychain**, the same secure storage macOS uses for your passwords. They aren’t saved in plain preference files, and each key is only ever sent to its own provider (your Claude key only to Anthropic, your OpenAI key only to OpenAI, and so on).

### Choosing a model

You pick the model in the chat, using the **model picker** next to the send button at the bottom of the AI Assistant panel. It lists the models of every provider you’ve turned on, each with a short hint on the right such as **Smartest**, **Balanced**, **Smart** or **Fast**.

At the top of the list is **Auto** (**Picks for you**). With Auto on, Woday picks a model for each message, based on the message itself:

- Short, quick commands (“add…”, “mark…”, “what’s…”) go to a fast, low-cost model.
- Everyday questions go to a balanced model.
- Bigger jobs go to the smartest model you have connected. That covers long messages, attached files, and words like “plan”, “summarize”, “rewrite”, “analyze” or “brainstorm”.

Woday makes this choice on your Mac. Auto doesn’t cost an extra request. If you attach a scanned PDF that has no text layer, Auto sends that message to a Claude model, because only Claude can read the scanned pages directly.

If a provider retires a model you had picked, Woday switches you to its replacement on its own.

## What gets sent to your AI provider, and when

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/ai-what-gets-sent

All guides

AI & MCP


Exactly what Woday sends to your AI provider, and the moments when it sends it.

Woday only contacts your AI provider when you use an AI feature. In each case, this is what goes to the provider:

- **Each message you send to Wodie.** This includes your message, the earlier messages in that chat, and a short snapshot of today: up to 8 events and 8 tasks for today, plus how many tasks are overdue. Facts you asked Wodie to remember and any files or items you attached are also included.
- **Anything Wodie looks up while answering.** When Wodie reads a note, searches your tasks or checks your calendar, the result of that lookup goes to the provider so the model can use it. Wodie only reads what your question needs.
- **Naming a chat.** After your first exchange in a new chat, a short excerpt of it goes to a fast model of the same provider to create the chat’s title.
- **Text tools and Magic Lines.** When you use AI writing tools in the editor or a Magic Lines answer, only the selected text or line (with a little context) is sent.
- **Agent mode** (if you turn it on). The same snapshot and lookups as above go out when Wodie opens a briefing, a heads-up or a review.

Your chat history stays on your Mac, in Woday’s own application folder. It is not synced through iCloud. The MCP server never sends anything anywhere: external AI apps connect to it on your Mac (see below).

## Setting up the MCP server

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/mcp-server-setup

All guides

AI & MCP


What MCP is, how to turn on Woday’s built-in MCP server and the connection details AI apps need.

**MCP (Model Context Protocol)** is an open standard that lets AI apps connect to other software through a shared set of “tools”. When you turn on Woday’s MCP server, AI apps like Claude Desktop, Cursor, Claude Code, Windsurf or VS Code can read and update your Woday tasks, notes and events. For example, you can ask Claude Desktop “what’s on my calendar tomorrow?” or have it file tasks into Woday as you talk.

The MCP server doesn’t need an AI key in Woday. The external app uses its own AI. Woday only provides the data and the tools.

### How to turn on the MCP server

1. Open **Settings ▸ AI Connections** and click the **MCP Server** card.
2. Turn on **MCP server**.
3. Check **Status**: it should read **Running** (green). It shows **Off** when the server is turned off, and **Can’t start** if something blocks it (see Troubleshooting).

The server is off by default. While it’s on, it runs whenever Woday is open. Connected apps can only reach Woday while it’s running.

### Connection details

- **Address**: `http://127.0.0.1:24680/mcp` (your Mac only)
- **Port**: `24680` by default. You can change it under **Advanced ▸ Port** (1024–65535; press Return to apply).
- **Authentication**: A Bearer token: every request must send `Authorization: Bearer <your access token>`
- **Transport**: Streamable HTTP (POST requests)

Your **access token** is created on its own the first time and stored in the macOS Keychain. The **Access token** row shows its first few characters with a **Copy** button. Under **Advanced** you can click the token to show it in full, copy the **Endpoint**, and generate a new token.

## MCP tools reference

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/mcp-tools-reference

All guides

AI & MCP


Every tool the MCP server offers: your day and calendar, tasks and habits, notes, search, organization and views, Trash, stats and more.

Dates use the format `YYYY-MM-DD` and times use `HH:mm` (24-hour), in your Mac’s time zone. Ids come from the tools’ own results. For update tools, only the fields you pass are changed, and `"none"` clears a field that can be cleared. The AI Assistant uses exactly the same tools.

### Your day and calendar

- **`get_agenda`**: Your day at a glance: scheduled tasks, events (including synced calendars) and the day note. For today it also lists overdue tasks. Key parameters: `date` (default today)
- **`list_events`**: Calendar events in a date range. Events from synced calendars are included and marked read-only. Key parameters: `from` (default today), `to` (default 7 days later)
- **`create_event`**: Creates an event. Without a start time it’s an all-day event. Key parameters: `title`, `date` (required); `start_time`, `end_time` (default 1 hour), `location`, `details`, `tags`, `reminder`, `folder`, `favorite`, `link_note`
- **`update_event`**: Changes an event: moves, retimes, renames or retags it, sets a reminder, or adds to its notes. ## AI and MCP security and privacy

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/ai-security-privacy

All guides

AI & MCP


How the local-only MCP server and Woday’s AI features keep your data under your control.

- **Local only.** The MCP server only accepts connections from your own Mac (127.0.0.1). Other computers on your network can’t reach it, and requests from web pages on other sites are refused.
- **Token required.** Every request must include your access token. Without it, Woday refuses the request.
- **New token.** If you think your token has leaked, open **Advanced** on the MCP Server page and click the **Generate a new token** button (the circular arrow). Every connected app is cut off until you give it the new token. Then click **Add to Claude Desktop…** or **Add to Cursor…** again, or copy the new command or JSON.
- **Off switch.** Turn off **MCP server** to stop all outside access right away.
- **Nothing is permanently deleted.** Neither Wodie nor MCP apps can permanently delete items. “Delete” always means moving to the Trash, and you can restore items from there.
- **Undo.** Changes Wodie makes in the chat can be undone with ⌘Z. Changes made by external MCP apps aren’t on Woday’s undo list, but deleted items can still be restored from the Trash.
- **Your keys, your data.** AI provider keys and the MCP token are stored in the macOS Keychain. Chats stay on your Mac. Data only goes to the AI provider you picked, when you use an AI feature.

AI & MCP


Common problems with AI connections and the MCP server, and what to do about each one.

- **The AI panel says **Set up the AI assistant****: Add a key under **Settings ▸ AI Connections ▸ AI Assistant** and make sure that provider’s switch is on.

## Apple Calendar

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/apple-calendar

All guides

Integrations


Connect Apple Calendar, add your Woday events to it two-way, set calendar colours and icons, and turn it off.

Woday reads your Apple Calendar events (iCloud, Exchange, Google accounts added in macOS, and so on) and can also write your own Woday events into Apple Calendar.

### How to connect Apple Calendar

1. Open **Settings ▸ Sync**.
2. Turn on **Apple Calendar**.
3. When macOS asks, allow Woday to access your calendars.
4. A list of your calendars appears below, grouped by account. Turn off any calendar you don’t want to see. New calendars are included automatically.

If you said no to the permission prompt, the caption reads **Calendar access is off.** Allow access in **System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Calendars**.

### Two-way: add your events to Apple Calendar

Turn on **Add my events to Apple Calendar** (just under the Apple Calendar switch).

- **Apple Calendar → Woday**: Events from the calendars you left on appear in all Woday calendar views, on Today and in the menu bar panel. Roughly the past month and the next three months are shown.
- **Woday → Apple Calendar (two-way only)**: Events you create or move in Woday appear in your real calendar and stay updated.

Good to know:

- **Woday is in charge of its own events.** If you edit a Woday event’s copy directly in the Calendar app, Woday overwrites that change on the next sync. Make your changes in Woday instead.
- **Events from Apple Calendar can’t be edited in Woday.** When you open one, you see its details: time, location, notes, guests and join links. Use **Open in Calendar** to change it or reply to the invitation.
- **You choose where new events go.** In **Settings ▸ General ▸ Default calendar for new events**, pick a calendar, or pick **Woday only** to keep new events local. You can also change the calendar for a single event from its details (the **+** menu ▸ **Calendar**, or the calendar pill once one is set). Only calendars you can write to are listed.
- **Tasks can appear on a calendar too.** You can assign a task to a calendar from the task’s details, and it will show up there as an event.

### Calendar colours and icons

Each synced calendar gets the Woday colour that’s closest to its real colour in Apple Calendar or Google Calendar, so things look familiar. To change a calendar’s look:

1. Find the calendar in **Settings ▸ Sync** (Apple), on the Google Calendar page, or under Calendar Feeds.
2. Click the coloured dot in front of the calendar’s name.
3. Pick a colour, and optionally a default icon for its events.

Your choice always wins over the automatic colour.

### How to turn Apple Calendar off

Turn off **Apple Calendar** in **Settings ▸ Sync**. Its events disappear from Woday. Nothing is deleted in Apple Calendar. To stop only the write-back, turn off **Add my events to Apple Calendar** and keep the main switch on.

## Apple Reminders

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/apple-reminders

All guides

Integrations


Bring your open Apple Reminders into Woday as real tasks, see what syncs, and group reminders into folders by list.

Your open Apple Reminders come into Woday as **real tasks**. You can plan them, drag them, open them, add notes and subtasks, and check them off like any other task.

### How to connect Apple Reminders

1. Open **Settings ▸ Sync**.
2. Turn on **Apple Reminders**.
3. When macOS asks, allow Woday to access your reminders.
4. Choose which lists to include from the list that appears below. New lists are included automatically.

If you said no to the permission prompt, allow access in **System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Reminders**.

**Note:** Woday can’t see which lists you’ve hidden inside the Reminders app. To leave out a list, turn it off in **Settings ▸ Sync**.

### What syncs

- **Reminders → Woday**: Open reminders arrive as tasks with their title, due date and time, priority and notes. A location reminder fills in the task’s place, and a timed alert becomes the task’s reminder. Words like `#work` in a reminder’s title or notes become Woday tags. Changes you make in Reminders (new title, new due date, completing it) flow in.
- **Woday → Reminders (two-way)**: With **Add my tasks to Apple Reminders** on, checking off an imported task completes the reminder too, and changes you make to its title or due date are written back. Tasks you create in Woday are added to your default Reminders list as well. Completion syncs both ways.

For the full round trip, where your Woday changes also reach the Reminders app, turn on **Add my tasks to Apple Reminders**.

Imported tasks have an **Apple Reminders** badge with the list’s name in their details.

### Group reminders into folders by list

Turn on **Group reminders into folders by list** to get a Woday folder for each of your Reminders lists:

- Each list opens as a Woday folder with the same name. Empty lists get a folder too.
- New reminders are filed into the matching folder automatically.
- When you turn it on, reminders you already imported are filed too, unless they’re already in a folder.
- If you move a task to another folder by hand, it stays where you put it.
- With **Add my tasks to Apple Reminders** on, a Woday task you put in one of these folders goes to the matching Reminders list.

### Turning lists or Reminders sync off

If you turn off a list, or turn off **Apple Reminders** completely, Woday moves the tasks it imported from those lists to the **Trash**, so you can still restore them. Your reminders in the Reminders app are never deleted by this. If you turn the list back on, the reminders come back in.

If you delete an imported task in Woday, it isn’t imported again.

## Apple Notes

> **Source:** https://www.woday.app/docs/apple-notes

All guides

Integrations


Bring in notes from the Apple Notes folders you choose, write changes back, and the limitations to know about.

Woday can bring in the notes from the Apple Notes folders you choose and can write your changes back. Paragraphs, headings, bulleted and numbered lists, checklists, tables and images become real Woday blocks, not just a block of text.

### How to connect Apple Notes

1. Open **Settings ▸ Sync**.
2. Turn on **Apple Notes**.
3. macOS asks whether Woday may control the Notes app. Click **Allow** (or **OK**). Woday opens Notes quietly in the background if it isn’t running.
4. Your Apple Notes folders appear below, grouped by account (for example iCloud or On My Mac). **All folders start switched off.** Turn on the ones you want to sync.
5. Each folder you turn on becomes a folder in Woday. A big first import shows a counter (for example `120 / 850`) next to the switch.

If you denied the permission, the caption reads **Automation access is off.** Turn on Woday ▸ Notes in **System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security ▸ Automation**, then switch Apple Notes off and on again.

### Write changes back to Apple Notes

By default, Woday only reads from Apple Notes. Turn on **Write changes back to Apple Notes** to make it two-way:

- Notes you edit in Woday are updated in Apple Notes.
- New notes you create inside a synced folder are created in Apple Notes too.
- If a note changes on both sides between syncs, the Woday version is kept.

### Limitations

- **Notes with scans, drawings or audio are read-only.** Woday can show their text and images but never writes to them, so Apple’s attachments can’t be damaged. These notes have an **Apple Notes · read-only** badge, and other synced notes have an **Apple Notes** badge.
- **Locked notes are skipped.**
- **Notes with file attachments added in Woday stay in Woday.** Apple Notes can’t hold Woday file attachments, so those notes aren’t written back. The same goes for notes that come from Notion or Obsidian.
- **Apple Notes checklists** come over as Woday to-dos. When written back, they appear in Notes as ☐ / ☑ lines, because Apple Notes doesn’t let other apps create real checklists.
- **Automatic updates run only while Notes is open.** Woday checks for changes every few minutes, but only when the Notes app is running, so it won’t keep opening Notes. Turning the sync on, or switching folders, always runs a full pass.
- **Only one Mac syncs at a time.** If you use Woday on several Macs with iCloud sync, one Mac handles Apple Notes and the others stay on standby so notes aren’t imported twice. The caption then reads **Apple Notes is syncing on “…”**. If that Mac goes offline, another one takes over automatically.

### Deleting notes

- **Woday never deletes anything in Apple Notes.** Deleting a synced note in Woday only removes it from Woday, and it isn’t imported again.
- If a note is deleted in Apple Notes (or moved out of a synced folder), its Woday copy goes to the **Trash**. If you had edited it in Woday, it isn’t trashed. It stays as a regular Woday note.
- If you turn off a folder or the whole sync, unchanged copies go to the Trash and notes you edited stay as regular Woday notes.

### Sample content

If Woday’s sample notes are still around while Apple Notes sync is on, you’ll see **Remove sample content** in the Apple Notes group. It deletes only Woday’s demo items, which have a **Sample** badge. It never touches Apple Notes.

### Other appearance options

- **Primary color**: The color used for selections, accents and highlights in every theme: Red (the default), Orange, Yellow, Green, Teal, Blue, Indigo, Purple, Pink or Graphite. Hover over a swatch to see its name. High priority uses this color too.

Appearance has two lists for arranging the sidebar.

**The sidebar menu** lists the pages: Upcoming, Calendar, Notes, Tasks, Events, Habits, Folders, Tags and Views. **Today** always stays first.

- **Favorites**: a page of everything you’ve starred.
- **Notes**: your newest notes.
- **Tasks**: the next open, dated tasks, soonest first. Off by default.
- **Events**: the next events, soonest first. Off by default.
- **Views**: your saved views.
- **Folders**: your folders, with their notes and tasks.
- **Tags**: your tags, which expand to the notes they mark.

To rearrange either list:

1. Open **Settings ▸ Appearance** and scroll to the menu list or the section list.
2. Use the **↑** and **↓** buttons to move an entry up or down.
3. Use the switch to show or hide it.

A hidden page can still be opened from search and links. A hidden section keeps its content; it just doesn’t take up space.
